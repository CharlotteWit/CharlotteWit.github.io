The challenge

Great science doesn't run itself.

Once the grant agreement is signed, the real work starts: partners in different countries, deliverables and milestones, periodic reports, budget rules, and a Project Officer who expects all of it on time. Scientists and founders are experts in their field, not in EU project administration, and they shouldn't have to be.

When nobody owns this work, you see it quickly:

Delays

Deliverables slip and milestones are missed.

Misalignment

Partners work in different directions, and decisions stall.

Reporting stress

Every reporting period becomes a last-minute scramble, and in the worst case part of the grant is at risk.

That's where I come in.

How I help

Project management for EU-funded projects

Start well

For projects that are just starting.

  • Kick-off meeting and governance structure
  • Planning of deliverables, milestones and reporting periods
  • A clear reporting structure that partners actually use

Keep it running, or get it back on track

For projects already underway.

  • A status check against your Grant Agreement, plus a recovery plan where needed
  • Continuous and periodic reporting in the EC portal
  • Milestone and budget tracking, and a risk register
  • Partner coordination and follow-up
  • Ownership of a specific work package, such as project management, dissemination & communication, or exploitation

A reporting tool built for your project

Alongside project management, I can set up a tracking and reporting tool tailored to your project: your deliverables, milestones and partners, in your own branding.

Proposal support

For teams preparing an EU funding application.

  • Consortium building and partner search
  • Work plan, work packages, milestones and budget planning
  • Collecting partner information (Part A)
  • Review cycles, steering the writing team, and submission via the Funding & Tenders portal

About me

Dr. Charlotte B. Wit

I'm a scientist turned project manager. After my PhD in Biology at Freie Universität Berlin (on neuronal connectivity and brain development), I spent three years as Scientific Project Manager at EU-OPENSCREEN ERIC. There I managed EU-funded projects with large international consortia, led work packages, prepared technical and financial reports, and presented results to Project Officers in Brussels.

Today I work as an independent consultant through Wit Science Consulting, supporting teams with EU project management and proposals.

I also develop custom digital tools that make project work simpler.
